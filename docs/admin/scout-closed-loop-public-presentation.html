Scout Closed Loop Operating Model

Scout is being shaped as a route-aware outdoor companion: it helps people plan before a trip, stay oriented and observable during the trip, and learn from completed trips without turning AI suggestions into unreviewed safety truth.

Operating model

Three loops turn Scout from a map viewer into a learning field system.

The product model is organized around three loops. Alpha should prove the first two. The third can land before release as the official transfer and ecosystem layer. Alpha must ship Loop 1 and Loop 2.

Alpha required 1. Climbing Experience Accumulation

Reviewed pre-trip plans, completed trip records, Capability Timeline, Energy Reserve, and next-plan suggestions. This loop does not trigger live safety or hardware actions.

Alpha foundation 2. On-Trip Scout Safe Device

Real plan-node check-ins, hardware sensing, Spatial Imprint playback, local cues, communication actions, and black-box evidence under runtime gates.

Release-before 3. Workspace Transfer And Ecosystem

Official Scout A to Scout B workspace template export/import, plus opt-in aggregate ecosystem contribution.

Alpha boundary

Alpha is not complete without the on-trip foundation.

The first loop proves Scout can learn from completed trips. The second loop proves Scout is useful while the user is still on the route. That second loop is the minimum on-trip Scout foundation.

Loop Alpha expectation What must stay bounded
Climbing Experience Accumulation Pre-trip reviewed plan, completed trip record, post-analysis in admin, Energy Reserve / Energy Limit feedback, next pre-trip candidates. Planning/post-analysis only: no live /safety/*, no hardware actuation, no real check-in/send, and no replayed action is executed again.
On-Trip Scout Safe Device Reviewed plan nodes, real hardware/software status, Ln action traces, physical check-ins, local cues, communication/team-care state, INS/PDR route generation, and search black-box refs. This is the real action loop, but actions remain gated by runtime handoff, Ln policy, operator confirmation, and provider policy.
Workspace Transfer And Ecosystem May wait until before broader release unless a specific alpha test needs Scout A to Scout B template transfer. Imported templates are candidate evidence only, not another user's runtime truth or capability profile.

Loop 1: before departure

Pre-trip turns fragmented route knowledge into a reviewed plan.

Source materialGPX, DEM/DTM, imagery, OSM/Overpass, notes, articles
Candidate evidenceCP, MCP, POI, hazards, terrain, risk, route notes
Pydantic AI review aidCluster, explain, flag conflicts, call deterministic tools
Human reviewAccept, reject, split, downgrade, or require more evidence
Reviewed packageRoute, CPs, policies, layers, metadata, handoff refs
MCP compresses dense CPs.

Major Critical Points give reviewers a human-scale layer above dense candidate points.

Terrain and risk stay separate.

Hillshade, slope, contours, and elevation tint are terrain evidence. Risk heat is route-specific candidate risk.

AI is synthesis, not authority.

Pydantic AI can explain and organize evidence, but reviewed artifacts decide what enters the package.

Loop 2: during the trip

Scout on-trip is a safe device loop, not just a recorder.

The reviewed plan becomes a live field structure: Scout watches for plan-node state, records hardware evidence, performs allowed physical/device actions, exposes why an action happened, and preserves black-box context.

Reviewed plan nodesCP, MCP, rest, hazard, communication, team-care
Hardware sensingGNSS, IMU, PDR, battery, radio, wearable, storage
Real actionsCheck-in, cue, Spatial Imprint playback, INS/PDR route update
Communication gateOperator-approved status, SOS, incident report, provider send
Black-box evidenceMinimal event package, last known node, trace, device status
Check-in

Plan-node arrival, departure, missed timing, and segment capsule state.

Team care

Regroup prompts, member-lag checks, rest needs, and buddy-check records.

Communication

Known signal points, relay opportunity, queued messages, and provider gates.

Search evidence

Local evidence refs for later retrieval without exposing private raw payloads by default.

Loop 1: after return

Post-analysis turns completed trips into future planning signal.

Capability Timeline is generated from a completed user track or Scout runtime record, not from public reference GPX. It produces segment rhythm, moving/rest/elapsed time, and confidence notes that can feed future planning after review.

  • Completed trip workspace is the source of personal capability evidence.
  • Energy Reserve describes baseline-relative margin.
  • Energy Limit converts that margin into conservative planning constraints.
  • Next pre-trip receives proposed rest, check-in, ETA buffer, or turnaround candidates.
Evidence only Not a medical device, not automatic approval.

Energy outputs can guide planning and field advisory cues, but they do not diagnose, rank people, or become runtime safety truth.

Loop 3: release-before

Workspace transfer makes Scout knowledge portable without copying trust.

A Scout workspace can become a richer template than public GPX: it can include Scout time, route notes, reviewed CP/MCPs, risk/terrain refs, and redaction policy. On import, it still becomes candidate evidence for the receiving Scout.

Safety and trust boundaries

The core rule: evidence can inform, review can promote, runtime must remain explicit.

Evidence Candidate-only by default

Pre-trip CP, MCP, terrain, risk, route-note, AI, and template imports start as evidence with provenance.

Review Human/AI-assisted review gates

Review can accept, reject, split, downgrade, or ask for more context. AI helps organize the work.

Runtime No silent mutation

Departure, runtime handoff, outbound sends, and incident reports stay explicit, auditable, and policy gated.

What has been specified

The closed loops sit on top of concrete specification work.

Planning intelligence

Pre-trip admin, historical GPX import, route-corridor preparation, Overpass/OSM, MCP synthesis, terrain visualization, and risk layers.

Runtime observability

Runtime readiness/debug tooling, admin GIS map operations, device stream admission, hardware status, and on-trip debug projection.

After-action learning

Capability Timeline, Energy Reserve / Energy Limit, companion match concepts, reviewed lessons, and future pre-trip suggestions.

Closing

Scout's public promise is an auditable outdoor operating loop.

Scout should help people prepare better, notice more during the route, and learn after return. The alpha milestone should prove the planning and on-trip loops. The broader release can then add official workspace transfer so route knowledge accumulates across the ecosystem without copying private data or unreviewed trust.

Public presentation draft. This document intentionally avoids raw route payloads, private health samples, incident contents, device credentials, and live safety activation details.
